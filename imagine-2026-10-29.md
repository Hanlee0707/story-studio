# Story Studio: how the BTS podcast canvas plays out

Status: active (imagining, 2026-10-29). Owner: Han. Goal: a showcase for the demo on 2026-10-30 where Little Tommy asks for a story, Han's backend makes a podcast of it, and the episode comes back into the same canvas with pictures.

Trail doc. Nothing here is built yet.

## The scene

Little Tommy opens Story Studio from Home. A studio desk, a mic, and one question in big type: "What story do you want tonight?" Three idea chips sit under it ("BTS at a sleepover", "Jimin and the lost puppy", "BTS go camping") and a hold-to-talk mic button.

She holds the mic and says "a story where BTS goes camping and Jimin is scared of the dark". The canvas shows what it heard. She can tap a word to fix it or add "and there is a bear". Then "Make my story".

The desk lights up and a record starts spinning. Sprout says one line: "Ooh. I'm sending that to the studio. It takes a minute or two." While she waits, the canvas shows the studio working, stage by stage, as the backend reports in: Writing the story, Picking the voices, Drawing the pictures, Mixing. Each stage lights up when it really happens, so the wait feels alive and nothing is faked.

A chime. The cover art slides in with a title the backend invented ("Jimin and the Night Forest"). One button: Listen.

Playback looks like a podcast player built for a kid: the scene picture fills the top, the current line of the story is printed under it, a progress bar, pause and a 10-second back button. The picture changes as the story moves to the next scene. At the end: "Make another" or Done. Done completes the run and pays the gems. Past episodes sit on a shelf at the bottom of the studio, so tomorrow night she can replay tonight's.

Parent side, optional for the demo: the episode also lands in Han's chat as a card, and Sprout can tell Han what she asked for.

## How each beat works on today's plumbing

1. Ask. `sprout.speech.listen` runs the phone's own recognizer, the same path Read Along uses. Typed fallback for the preview. `sprout.whoami` gives the kid's id for her data path.

2. Send. The canvas writes one document to the canvas db: `data/users/<kid>/requests/<id>` with the prompt, the time, and `status: asked`. The canvas never needs network for this. (A `sprout.net.fetch` GET to a Vercel function could also kick the job off, as Read Along does for Claude answers, but the result cannot come back that way: text is capped at 64K characters and audio is not allowed.)

3. Pick it up. A small worker on Han's Mac holds the stage MCP token and polls `canvas_list_documents` on the requests collection every few seconds (`resources_wait_many` may long-poll a db uri; to check). It claims the request by setting `status: writing`. Each later stage is one more `canvas_write_documents` on the same doc, which is what lights the stages in the canvas.

4. Generate. Claude writes the script as scenes: speaker, line, and a picture prompt per scene, about 90 to 120 seconds of audio. TTS per speaker (a narrator plus a guest voice; BTS as characters in generic voices, never cloned voices of real people). Stitch with a music bed into one mp3. One picture per scene plus a cover, four to six images. The story text that goes into the db is text-moderated on write, so the script stays wholesome by construction.

5. Put it back. `canvas_prepare_upload` in bundle mode: the unchanged `index.html` plus `episodes/<id>/audio.mp3` and `episodes/<id>/scene-N.jpg` (mp3 and jpeg are on the bundle allowlist; 10 MB per asset, 25 MB per bundle). Signed PUTs, then `canvas_get_scan_status` until the malware scan says clean, then `canvas_update` with the `blobRef`. The Song family authored the canvas, so the new version auto-approves. Then `canvas_write_documents`: `episodes/<id>` with title, cover path, audio path, scenes with their start times and lines, and the request flips to `status: ready`. The bundle grows one episode at a time; at 64 kbps a two-minute mp3 is about 1 MB, so roughly twenty episodes fit before pruning.

6. Hear it. The canvas subscribes with `onSnapshot` on the request doc, so the stages and the ready state arrive without polling. The audio tag points at the bundle path; the sandbox CSP allows `media-src 'self' data: blob:` so bundle audio plays. Scene pictures swap on `timeupdate` against the scene start times.

## The catch, and two ways around it

The audio lives in the new version's bundle, and the page she is looking at is the old version. A version bump resets the run, so the next open renders the new version, but the open page cannot swap itself. For tomorrow the ready state can say "Your story is ready. Tap Done and open Story Studio again", or Sprout nudges her, and the reopen takes two taps. Fine for a demo, worth fixing properly later.

Two ways to avoid the reopen, both to test tonight before relying on them:

- Audio in the db. Split the mp3 into base64 chunks under `episodes/<id>/audio/<n>` (256 KB per document) and join them into a blob URL in the page. No version bump, arrives live over onSnapshot. Unknown: db writes send every string through the text classifier, and 190K characters of base64 per chunk may be refused or slow.
- Script only. Skip generated audio; the canvas reads the script line by line with `sprout.tts.speak` in Sprout's voice over the pictures. Instant and no binary at all, but one voice and no music. This is also the safety net if generation stalls on stage during the demo.

## Timing and a safety net

Script about 15 s, voices 20 to 30 s, pictures 30 to 60 s run in parallel, upload and scan 10 to 20 s. Expect 90 s to 2 min from "Make my story" to the chime. Pre-bake one episode tonight so the demo opens on "last night's story" that plays instantly, and run the live request on top of it.

## Decisions for Han

- Where the backend runs. A worker on the Mac at the demo (recommended: it can poll, run for minutes, and hold the API keys) versus Vercel (no 3-second polling, generation over 60 s needs background functions).
- Voices. Narrator telling a BTS story, or BTS as speaking characters in generic voices. No cloned voices of real people either way.
- Accept the reopen for tomorrow, or spend tonight on audio-in-db.
- Family: Song family, Little Tommy on stage, like Read Along. The kid persona is Han's daughter.

## Han's answer: no bundle, the episode goes up as canvas data

Han (2026-10-29 evening): "can it just go up as the canvas data into DB instead?" Measured on stage against the Read Along canvas store (writes to `scratch/…`, deleted after):

| Write | Result |
|---|---|
| one document, 187K chars of base64 | refused in 1.6 s, "content checks are unavailable" (47 moderation chunks of 4000 chars, cap is 20 per request) |
| one document, 60K / 30K / 8K chars | accepted, about 3 s each |
| ten 60K documents in one batch call | 11 s, 6 accepted, 4 unavailable (provider rate limit) |
| ten 60K documents one call at a time, retry on unavailable | all landed in 65 s with 12 retries |

So yes. Rules that fall out of it: at most 80K characters of base64 per document (20 chunks of 4000; 60K leaves margin), one write per call with a retry on `unavailable`, and expect roughly a minute per 600K characters because every chunk goes through the text classifier. A document holds 256 KB, so the moderation cap binds first, not the document cap.

Shape of an episode in the store, under `episodes/<id>`:

- `meta`: title, status (writing, voicing, drawing, ready), scene list with each scene's line and durations. Small, text, moderated for real.
- `cover`: one jpeg at about 40 KB as base64 (one document).
- `scenes/<n>/audio`: one mp3 per scene, 15 to 25 s at 32 kbps mono, 60 to 100 KB, so one or two documents each (`scenes/<n>/audio/<k>` when split).
- `scenes/<n>/art`: one jpeg per scene, one document.

Per scene audio is the trick: the page plays scene 1 as soon as its documents land while the rest keep arriving, so the kid starts listening about 20 s after the script is written instead of waiting for a whole upload. The page joins the base64 into a Blob and sets it as the audio source; the sandbox CSP allows `blob:` for media. No canvas version bump, no reopen, the whole thing rides `onSnapshot`.

Follow-up for Sprout proper, not for tomorrow: a binary field type in the canvas db (base64 validated, not sent to the text classifier) so audio and pictures stop costing moderation calls and the rate limit stops shaping the upload.
